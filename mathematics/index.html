Mathematics & Statistics

Recreational mathematics, probability, Bayesian modelling and statistical explanations by Sean Elvidge, from puzzles to football and extreme space weather.

Much of this website begins with a deceptively simple question and ends with a probability distribution, a model, or an unexpectedly long calculation. This collection brings together recreational mathematics, statistics, Bayesian modelling and puzzles, with routes into their applications in football and space weather.

Probability and statistical thinking

  • The birthday problem — why shared birthdays become likely surprisingly quickly.
  • Bayes’ theorem: Maths on a Mug 15 — updating probabilities when new evidence arrives.
  • An accessible introduction to extreme value theory — the statistics of rare events, including severe space weather.
  • Are all World Cup group draws equally likely? — how constraints change probabilities.

Bayesian models and forecasting

My Kalman filter resources and tutorial notes connect Bayes’ theorem to estimating a changing system from noisy observations. Try the bouncing-ball example, then see the same family of ideas used in football team-strength modelling.

For a model-validation example, read how I compared Euro 2020 predictions alongside the final results. A forecast needs to be judged by more than whether one memorable prediction came true.
